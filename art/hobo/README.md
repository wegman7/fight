# Hobo model

The drunk hobo (`Character::Hobo`): a drunken-fist brawler. A gaunt, weathered drifter
with a bulbous red drinker's nose, bleary heavy-lidded eyes and a full, unkempt beard,
in a slouchy knit beanie, a layered, tattered overcoat (frayed hem and cuffs, moth holes,
plaid patches, mismatched buttons) over a sweater, saggy patched trousers held up with a
rope, fingerless gloves and worn-out shoes (one untied, a sock poking through the toe).
He carries a green glass bottle in his near hand and pulls a beat-up shopping cart out of
thin air for his side special. Rigged and animated in Blender and exported to
`godot/models/hobo.glb`. His moveset is in the sim (`sim/src/character.rs`, frame data at
the end of `sim/src/moves.rs`; the root README lists the moves).

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `build_hobo.py` | Generates the whole asset from code: mesh, armature, every animation, the textures. Saves `hobo.blend` and exports the `.glb`. |
| `hobo.blend` | The Blender file. Open it to look at or edit the model by hand. |
| `export_hobo.py` | Exports `hobo.blend` to `godot/models/hobo.glb` (same glTF settings as the chef). |
| `hobo_*.png` | The baked textures (see below), regenerated by every build. Godot extracts its own copies as `godot/models/hobo_hobo_*.png` on import. |

```sh
blender --background --factory-startup --python art/hobo/build_hobo.py      # rebuild from the script
blender --background art/hobo/hobo.blend --python art/hobo/export_hobo.py   # or export hand edits
godot --headless --path godot --import                                        # then reimport
```

As with the chef, rebuilding from the script overwrites hand edits to `hobo.blend`.
Choose one approach per change.

## Built on the chef

He stands on the chef's skeleton (same joints, 1.2 m tall; the beanie rises above it),
so `build_hobo.py` imports `art/fighter/build_fighter.py` for the metaball shapes
(`Blob`), the bmesh helpers (`tube`, `ring_band`, `uv_ellipsoid`) and the pose helpers
(`guard`, `crouch`, `walk_key`, `leg_ik`, `planted`, `with_`, `AIR`, `FLINCH`). Changing
those in the chef's script changes the hobo too, so rebuild both. The chef's pose
convention applies (see
[`art/fighter/README.md`](../fighter/README.md#pose-convention-in-build_fighterpy)).

The rules in [`art/fighter/README.md`](../fighter/README.md#rules-the-game-relies-on)
apply here too, except for the move names: the hobo needs the shared state animations
(`STATE_ANIMS`, including the four ledge clips below), the dodge animations and one per
hobo move (`Character::Hobo.moves()` — all 27 of them, each prefixed `hobo_`, since he
doesn't share the chef's grab). The game logs an `ERROR` at startup for any it can't
find. `godot/models/hobo.glb.import` must keep `animation/fps=60`.

## The props

- **The `bottle` bone** (child of `forearm.R`) runs along +X through the right fist,
  like the samurai's katana: a red cap, a green glass neck, shoulder and body, and a
  paper label round the body. `bottle_in(pose, facing)` shows it in the fist and aims its
  neck (`0` = forward, `90` = up); every other action keys its scale to 0, so it never
  lingers. It shows during `hobo_bottle_swing`, `hobo_up_heave`, `hobo_bottle_throw`
  (where it vanishes on frame 12 as the sim fires the projectile) and `ledge_attack`.
- **The `cart` bone** (child of `hips`, in front of him) carries the shopping cart:
  a wire basket of two hoops and slanting bars, a red handle, four legs, four caster
  wheels and an under-tray. `cart_in(pose)` shows it, keyed only during
  `hobo_cart_charge`; everywhere else its scale is 0.

## Ledge animations

`ledge_hang`, `ledge_climb`, `ledge_roll` and `ledge_attack` are generated per frame by
`getup()` from sparse control keys, against the sim's ledge geometry
(`try_grab_ledge`, `ledge_getup_pos` and the `LEDGE_*` constants in
`sim/src/fighter.rs`, `LEDGE_ATTACK_HITBOX` in `sim/src/world.rs`):

- While hanging, the origin is 0.1 m outside the edge and 1.2 m (his height) below the
  stage's top, so the ledge's corner is at model (0.1, 1.2). The keys are written in that
  hang frame; during a getup of N frames the sim slides the origin linearly
  (0.1 m + `LEDGE_*_IN`) inward and 1.2 m up, and `getup()` subtracts that per frame
  (`GETUPS` holds each clip's frames and inward distance).
- Arms and legs are either given as angles or solved onto a target with IK (`arm_ik`,
  `leg_ik`), which is how the hands stay planted on the corner while the body hoists
  under them, and feet stay planted on the stage.
- `ledge_hang` loops over 72 frames (a drunk's pendulum sway, lazy kicks, lolling head).
  `ledge_climb` (20) hauls up, gets a foot on the stage and lurches two steps in.
  `ledge_roll` (25) hoists and does a tucked, wobbling forward roll (a full -360° turn of
  the hips) along the stage, popping up in a stagger. `ledge_attack` (30) hoists, pulls
  out the bottle and smashes it down through the sim's hitbox on frames 15-18. Each
  getup ends exactly on the `idle` pose.
- `fk()` gives the side-view joint positions of any pose, for checking hand/corner
  contact or reach without rendering.

## Materials and textures

`Body` is the sweater under the coat (its front and the ribbed cuffs and collar showing
at the wrists), which gets the player's colour and state tints in game; nothing else
does. The rest keep their own colours: `Coat`, `Lining` (the dark of the moth holes),
`Patch`, `Thread`, `Skin`, `Ruddy` (the drinker's nose), `Lip`, `Hair`, `Beard`, `Grey`
(grey whiskers), `Eye`, `Iris`, `Pupil`, `Beanie`, `Glove`, `Pants`, `Rope`, `Shoe`,
`Sole`, `Lace`, `Sock`, `Button`, `Brass`, `Bottle`, `Label`, `Cap`, `Cart`,
`CartWheel` and `CartTrim`.

`make_textures()` bakes seeded, tileable value-noise maps with numpy (identical on every
rebuild) to `hobo_*.png`, and `TEXTURES` wires them into the materials as image
textures so they survive the glTF export (procedural Blender nodes would be dropped):

| Material | Maps |
|---|---|
| `Coat` | wool twill albedo with faded patches and greasy grime stains, roughness (grime is shinier), twill/fibre normal |
| `Skin`, `Ruddy` | weathered albedo (blotches, broken veins, sun spots, dirt), pore/crease normal |
| `Beard`, `Hair` | strand streaks, the beard salted with grey, and a streak normal |
| `Beanie`, `Glove` | stocking-stitch knit albedo and normal |
| `Pants` | worn cotton drill albedo and normal |
| `Patch` | faded plaid flannel albedo, with the drill normal |

Each textured material repeats its maps through one Mapping node, exported as
`KHR_texture_transform` (Godot applies it as the material's UV1 scale). The UVs are one
`smart_project` over the whole mesh at about 3.6 m of surface per UV unit, so a `tile`
of 8 repeats the map every 0.45 m. Keep low-frequency variation in the maps subtle: the
UV islands' seams show wherever a map changes a lot over a few centimetres.

## How it's built

- **Cloth and flesh** are metaball surfaces, one per piece: the gaunt head (cheekbones,
  hollow cheeks and temples, eye sockets with bags under them, nostril wings, creases from
  nose to mouth, lips, ears with a lobe), the lank hair, the beard (with ragged clumps
  hanging off it), the overcoat's body (hunched back, open front) and each baggy sleeve,
  the sweater, each hand (three-jointed, slightly curled fingers and a thumb) and the
  fingerless glove over it, each saggy trouser leg (low seat, bagged knee, slack pooled
  in folds at the ankle, the far knee torn) and each shoe upper (toe creases; a hole in
  the near one's toe).
- **Details** are bmesh parts placed by ray casting or fitted to those surfaces: the eyes
  (eyeball, iris, pupil) inside lid shells with an almond opening, lid rims and lashes;
  the brows, ear rims and stray beard, brow and hair strands (`strands`); the ribbed,
  rolled-cuff beanie fitted over head and hair; the coat's skirt (a solidified sheet
  hanging open to the knees, split at the back, with a tattered hem, holes cut through
  it, frayed threads, plaid patches with running stitches and mismatched buttons); the
  turned-up collar and rolled lapels; frayed sleeve cuffs over the sweater's ribbed
  cuffs; elbow and knee patches and moth holes conforming to the cloth (`decal`); the
  rope belt; each shoe's sole and welt, criss-cross laces (the near one untied and
  trailing) and the sock poking through; the bottle and the cart.
- **Skinning**: the skirt's top follows the hips and lower down each side follows its
  thigh, like the chef's apron. Sewn-on details take the weights of the nearest vertex of
  the cloth they sit on (`weights_near`).
